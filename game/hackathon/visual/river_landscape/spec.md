### Specification for AI agent: River landscape

Replaces the `river_bed` task. That one dropped `RiverFoam.glb` onto the
checkerboard as a prop; this one replaces the checkerboard itself. The floor
stops being a readout and becomes a place: flat grass where the game is
played, hills and wildflowers around it, a river off to one side, and
snow-capped mountains on the horizon.

The model is `/models/river_landscape.glb`, generated by
`tools/generate_river_landscape.mjs` (no dependencies, deterministic seed,
re-runnable with `node tools/generate_river_landscape.mjs`). It is 24,982
triangles and 2.6 MB. Everything is flat-shaded with baked vertex colours —
no textures and no image files, so there is nothing to load alongside it.

#### What the model guarantees

Two numbers make this safe to stand on, and both are enforced in the
generator rather than assumed here:

- **The middle is dead flat.** Every vertex within 17 model units of the
  origin sits at exactly `y = 0`, and the first non-flat vertex is at 17.25.
  At the import scale below that is a flat disc of radius 707 world units.
  Enemies spawn at the four `(±220, ±220)` corners — 311 from the origin —
  and houses are placed within 170, so both sit well inside it.

  This disc is only as useful as the bound that keeps Tode on it, and there
  is currently no such bound — see *Keeping Tode on the flat* below.
- **The river never enters the play area.** The channel's centre line is
  pushed out to roughly 30.5 model units, and the nearest water vertex to the
  origin is at 26.6 — about 2657 world units out. It is scenery seen across
  the fields, not something Tode can reach the bank of.

If either constant is changed, change it in the generator (`FLAT_R`,
`BLEND_R`, `riverX`) and regenerate; do not scale or reposition the model to
compensate, because that breaks the other one.

The model carries two materials, and the names matter because the import code
keys off them:
- `Landscape` — opaque, the ground and everything growing on it.
- `Water` — `alphaMode: BLEND` at 0.78 opacity.

#### Landscape

In the `components` directory, add a `Landscape.js` file:

- Export `createLandscape()`, returning a `THREE.Group` and loading
  `/models/river_landscape.glb` through `GLTFLoader`, the same way
  `House.js` does.
- Set `MODEL_SCALE = 41`, giving a terrain sheet 3280 world units across,
  the first tree at 922 and the mountains at 1927.

  This number is set by the camera, not by the old floor. `Camera.js` can
  see about 2500 units out (see below); at a scale of 100 the nearest tree
  stood at 2250 and the mountains at 4700, so every piece of scenery in the
  model sat outside the frustum and the game read as bare green ground with
  nothing on it. Sizing the landscape to what the camera can reach is the
  whole point of the value.
- Set `model.rotation.x = Math.PI / 2`. The `.glb` is Y-up and this game is
  Z-up, the same quarter turn `House.js` and `Player.js` already apply.
- Drop the model to `z = -0.05`. The basin is at model `y = 0`, so after the
  rotation the flat ground lands exactly on the plane Tode, the enemies and
  the houses all stand on — which is the coplanar case the old floor's
  `FLOOR_Z = -0.05` existed to avoid. The floor is gone but the reason is
  not: entity undersides still sit at exactly zero, so keep the offset and
  keep the comment explaining it.
- Traverse the loaded scene and set `receiveShadow = true` on every `Mesh`,
  and `castShadow = true` on every mesh except the one whose
  `material.name === 'Water'`. A transparent plane casting a shadow reads as
  a dark slab on the riverbed.
- Loading is asynchronous, so the world is empty for the first few frames.
  That is the same deal `House.js` already offers and needs no handling.

#### Keeping Tode on the flat

`Movement.js` does not bound the player. `JUMP_DISTANCE` is 80 and nothing
in `updateMovement` tests the landing point against a world edge, so Tode can
hop as far as the arrow keys are held — the 3200-unit floor only ever made
leaving it *unrewarding*, not impossible, and `Map.js` says as much
("far enough that Tode cannot casually wander off the edge"). Against a
checkerboard that stretched to the horizon this never showed. Against real
terrain it does: twenty-two hops in one direction and he is standing inside
a hill, at `z = 0`, with the ground drawn through his waist.

In `Movement.js`:
- Add `const WORLD_RADIUS = 700;` next to `JUMP_DISTANCE`, with a comment
  tying it to `FLAT_R` in the generator and the scale in `Landscape.js`.
  These are the same fact stated in three places and will drift otherwise.
  The play area is then 1400 units across, about 315 ft — down from the
  checkerboard's 3200, which is the price of having scenery close enough to
  see.
- In `updateMovement`, after `nextX`/`nextY` are computed and before the
  enemy test, cancel the hop if `Math.hypot(nextX, nextY) > WORLD_RADIUS`.
  Reuse `cancelMove(player)` — a hop into the edge of the world should fail
  the same way a hop into a house does, so it reads as the same rule rather
  than a new one.
- Put the check before the enemy collision test, not after. An enemy that has
  chased Tode to the rim should not be able to kill him on a hop that was
  never going to happen.

Enemies need no equivalent bound: they spawn at 311 from the origin and home
on the player, so a bounded player keeps them bounded.

#### Tode's scale

Against a checkerboard Tode had nothing to be the wrong size next to. Against
houses, pines and a river he does: at `MODEL_SCALE = 20` he measures 20 x 15
world units on the ground and 12.6 tall, which next to an 18 ft cottage makes
him a four-and-a-half-foot frog.

In `Player.js`:
- Change `MODEL_SCALE` from `20` to `16`.

That puts him at 16 x 12 on the ground and 10.1 tall — about knee height, or
2.3 ft on the house's scale. Nothing else in `Player.js` changes: the model
is already lifted by `-box.min.z` measured after scaling, so it keeps resting
exactly on the ground plane on its own.

16 is a second pass. The first was 12, chosen purely against the houses, and
it did not survive the camera: the wider field of view the scenery needs
costs Tode screen size, and at 12 he came out an eleven-pixel speck. 16 is
what keeps him at roughly the 30 px he had before while still reading as a
frog rather than a bus.

Two consequences, both wanted:
- A hop goes 5 body widths instead of 4. Frogs cover something like ten
  body lengths in a jump, so the smaller Tode reads more like a frog in
  motion and less like a bus changing lanes.
- The monsters get relatively bigger. `enemy.glb` at `MODEL_SCALE = 5` is
  21.5 units across and does not change, so it stays a third wider than Tode
  instead of matching him. A thing that is chasing you should be bigger than
  you are.

One consequence to watch: `Collision.js` measures the footprint off the
model, so Tode's collision radius drops from 8.8 to 7.05. With the enemy's
7.9 and `MIN_COLLISION_DISTANCE` of 8, the Game Over clearance goes from 24.7
units to 23.0 — a 7% smaller kill radius, so dodging gets slightly easier.
That is a tuning change nobody asked for, and if it is felt in play, correct
it with `MIN_COLLISION_DISTANCE` rather than by growing Tode back. It is a
flat constant and is now the largest of the three terms, which makes it the
honest knob for how much personal space characters keep.

Do not reduce `JUMP_DISTANCE` to match. It was pinned to `TILE_SIZE` so one
hop read as one square, and this task deletes the squares, so the constraint
is genuinely gone — but 80 is also what `WORLD_RADIUS` and the enemy spawn
ring were sized around, and re-pitching the whole game's travel distance is
not this task.

#### Camera

The scenery is useless if the camera cannot point at it, and as it stands it
cannot. `Camera.js` sits at `(300, -300, 300)` relative to the player with a
30 degree field of view, which pitches it 35 degrees down and puts the *top*
edge of the frustum 20 degrees **below** horizontal. The horizon is never in
frame, and the furthest ground the view can reach is 388 units from Tode. Any
landscape beyond that is invisible wherever he stands.

In `Camera.js`:
- Change `FIELD_OF_VIEW` from `30` to `45`.
- Change `CAMERA_POSITION` from `{ x: 300, y: -300, z: 300 }` to
  `{ x: 260, y: -260, z: 180 }`.

Lowering the camera is what does most of the work, and it pays twice: a
shallower pitch (26 degrees, down from 35) tips the frustum's top edge out
towards the horizon, and the shorter distance to Tode makes him *larger* on
screen rather than smaller. Together the camera now sees about 2500 units
out, which covers the trees at 922 and the mountains at 1927 with room over.

Do not fix this by raising the camera or by widening the field of view alone.
Raising it steepens the pitch and costs reach; widening it past about 55
degrees puts visible barrel distortion at the edges and shrinks Tode faster
than it reveals ground.

#### Map

`Map.js` currently builds the checkerboard. It keeps its job — returning the
group everything else parents to — and loses the pattern:

- Delete `createCheckerboardTexture()`, `DARK_GREEN`, `LIGHT_GREEN` and the
  `PlaneGeometry` floor. The landscape is the ground now, and a plane under
  it would either z-fight with the flat basin or show through the riverbed,
  which is carved to about 212 world units below zero.
- Keep `createMap()` exporting a `THREE.Group`, and call `createLandscape()`
  from it, adding the result to that group. The houses are already parented
  to the map group, so they keep travelling with the ground they sit on and
  `main.js` needs no change beyond what it already does.
- `TILE_SIZE` and `GRID_SIZE` can go with the floor. Nothing else imports
  them — `Movement.js` carries its own `JUMP_DISTANCE = 80`.

#### Constraints

- **Hop distance stops being legible.** The checkerboard doubled as a readout
  of how far one key press moves Tode, and flat grass does not. This is the
  accepted cost of the task, not an oversight. Do not solve it by tinting the
  terrain in squares; if it turns out to matter in play, it is a separate
  task and belongs in its own spec.
- **The two greens in the original `spec.md` no longer apply.** The ground's
  colour now comes from vertex data in the model. To change it, edit the
  `GRASS` / `GRASS_DARK` constants in the generator and regenerate.
- **Scale, for anyone eyeballing it.** Anchoring on the house, which is 80
  world units tall and reads as a small single-storey cottage of roughly
  18 ft, one world unit is about 2.7 inches. That puts one hop at 18 ft,
  Tode at 2.3 ft tall, the play basin at 315 ft across, the first trees 207 ft
  out, the mountains 434 ft away, and the whole landscape 738 ft across. The
  play field is roughly one football field.
- The camera's `FAR_PLANE` of 9000 is now far more than needed — the whole
  landscape is 3280 across. Leave it; an oversized far plane costs nothing
  here, and tightening it is a depth-precision tweak this task does not need.
- If the `atmosphere` task is also applied, its `THREE.Fog(..., 600, 2400)`
  happens to suit this landscape almost exactly — the terrain is 3280 across
  and the mountains sit at 1927, so a `far` of 2400 dissolves the horizon
  just behind them. Check `near`: at 600 it starts fogging well inside the
  700-unit play area, so raise it to around 900 to keep the field clear.
- Keep the river scenery. It does not take part in collision, and
  `Collision.js` is not touched by this task.

#### Research

The flat basin is a compromise: the game is planar and the model is not, so
the interesting ground is all pushed outside where Tode can walk. Worth
asking whether `Movement.js` could sample terrain height and let him hop up
into the hills, which would put the river, the banks and the pines back in
play — and would need the enemies, the collision tests and the camera to
follow him in Z.
